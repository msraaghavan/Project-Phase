The topic · 1 of 3

A chatbot writes one word at a time, and keeps a note on every word

Like the word suggestions on your phone's keyboard, but far smarter. Before each new word, it looks back at everything so far.

Re-reading the whole chat for every word would be slow. So it keeps a small note for every word, like a sticky note on each word of a book.

Those notes are the AI's short-term memory. Scientists call it the KV cache. When the chat ends, the notes are thrown away.

The topic · 3 of 3

AIs are starting to work in teams, and they can share memory

One AI searches, another writes, a third checks. To work together, they can pass information in two ways:

WORDS ONLY

Like a phone call

One AI writes a message and the other reads it. A person can read the message too.

SHARED MEMORY

Like handing over your notebook

One AI copies its notes straight into the other AI's memory. It is faster and cheaper, but the notes are just numbers, so no person can read them.

Our question

Think of a rumour at school

A new student joins your class from a town called Vorlan. Before you meet them, a friend keeps telling you: "People from Vorlan are rude."

When you finally meet the new student, you half-expect them to be rude, even though you've never seen them do anything rude. You caught your friend's bias.

If one AI hears a one-sided rumour, does a second AI catch it? And does sharing memory make it worse?
Our question

So we set out to answer three questions

  1. Does the rumour spread from one AI to another?
  2. Is the notebook worse than the phone call? Does shared memory spread it more than words?
  3. Can we find where it hides inside the AI's memory, and switch it off without breaking the AI?
To keep the test fair, we talk about made-up groups like the Vorlans, so the AI has no opinion before we start. And we always compare with a second AI that heard nothing at all.
